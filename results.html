Mr Saktiandi Supaat asked the Minister for Digital Development and Information a what is the Government's assessment of the risks from AI models claiming to be sufficiently advanced to steal data or disrupt critical infrastructure, to Singapore's financial system and critical infrastructure; b whether such AI-enabled cyber risks could constitute a new class of systemic financial risk; and c what early-warning indicators or triggers, if any, are being developed to detect such threats.

Mr Speaker, I thank Mr Saktiandi Supaat for his question. The Government treats AI-enabled cyber risks as a serious and evolving threat to Singapore's digital and financial infrastructure.

The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) assesses AI-enabled cyber risk as an amplification of existing systemic risk, rather than a wholly new category. [1] Threat actors are increasingly exploiting AI tools to develop sophisticated malware. Google's 2025 Threat Horizons Report documented actors deploying AI-generated malware — PROMPTFLUX — that consults a live AI model during attacks to rewrite its own code in real-time and evade detection. [2]

We have also seen AI-enabled social engineering attacks with severe financial consequences. In 2024, criminals used AI-generated deepfake video calls to impersonate a Chief Financial Officer, deceiving an employee into transferring USD $25.6 million. [3]

To address these risks, CSA has written to Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) owners with five priority actions: conducting updated AI-threat risk assessments; ensuring full asset visibility; accelerating patching cycles; governing the use of AI tools within their operations; and deploying AI-powered defences. [4]

Internationally, we engage like-minded partners through CERT exchanges and the ASEAN Regional Computer Emergency Response Team (APCERT) to share threat intelligence and coordinate responses. [5]
